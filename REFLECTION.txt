The two solutions are very similar and functionally equivalent. The most notable difference is that your solution makes doRun() and doEvaluate() public, while mine are protected. Since run() and evaluate() are final, I think public overrides may let outside code bypass those wrappers. Your operator switch also throws an UnsupportedOperationException() with a message in the default case, while mine just throws a AssertionError(). You also have a comment in the empty catch block, which mine doesn't. I think the message and the comment are good coding practices.
